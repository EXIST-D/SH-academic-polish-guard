# Effect of soft substrates on osteoblast mineralisation

## Abstract

With advances in tissue engineering, researchers are increasingly interested in substrate stiffness.  In this study we cultured primary rat osteoblasts on polyacrylamide gels of 1, 10 and 40 kPa for 21 days. The mineralised nodule area on 10 kPa gels was 2.3-fold higher than that on 40 kPa gels (p = 0.004, n = 6). Nuclear YAP localisation was lower on soft gels, which suggests that YAP signalling may mediate part of the stiffness response [3]. The effect was not observed in a human cell line, so the results cannot be generalised to all cell types.

## Introduction

Substrate stiffness is known to influence stem cell fate [1,2]. Engler et al. showed that matrix elasticity directs lineage specification of mesenchymal stem cells [2]. However, direct tests on primary osteoblasts are scarce. Because the osteoblast dataset is small, we used many replicates. The dataset was described in Table 1.

## Methods

Primary osteoblasts were isolated from the calvaria of neonatal Sprague-Dawley rats and cultured in an osteogenic medium. Cells were seeded at 5,000 cells per cm2 on gels with Young's modulus of 1, 10 or 40 kPa. Mineralised nodules were stained with Alizarin Red S and the stained area was quantified in six wells per condition (Fig. 2). To evaluate YAP, nuclear localisation was scored by immunofluorescence in at least 200 nuclei per gel. The YAP inhibitor verteporfin was dosed at 1 μM. We analyse the data with a one-way ANOVA and a post-hoc test.

## Results

Mineralised nodule area on 10 kPa gels was 2.3-fold higher than on 40 kPa gels. The fraction of nuclei with nuclear YAP was 31% on 10 kPa gels and 58% on 40 kPa gels. Verteporfin reduced nodule area on 40 kPa gels by 22%, but did not change it on 10 kPa gels. These results show that YAP is important for the stiffness response. In Saos-2 cells nodule area did not differ between the stiffnesses, which is an obvious limitation.

## Discussion

Our findings are completely different from the opinion that stiffer matrices always promote osteogenic differentiation. We did not test in vivo scaffolds, but this will be a breakthrough in the future. All experiments used cells from a single litter, so donor variability was not assessed. The CNN was not used here. The convolutional neural network (CNN) is mentioned only in Fig. 4 and Table 2.
