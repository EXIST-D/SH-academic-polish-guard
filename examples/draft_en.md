# Effect of soft substrates on osteoblast mineralisation

## Abstract

With the development of tissue engineering, more and more researchers pay attention to the substrate stiffness.  In this study we cultured primary rat osteoblasts on polyacrylamide gels of 1, 10 and 40 kPa for 21 days. The mineralised nodule area on 10 kPa gels were 2.3-fold higher than that on 40 kPa gels (p = 0.004, n = 6). Nuclear YAP localization was lower on soft gels, which suggests that YAP signaling may mediate a part of the stiffness response [3]. The effect was not observed in a human cell line , so the results can not be generalised to all cell types.

## Introduction

It is well known that substrate stiffness plays an important role in stem cell fate [1,2]. Engler et al. showed that matrix elasticity directs lineage specification of mesenchymal stem cells [2]. However, direct tests on primary osteoblasts is scarce. Because the osteoblast data set is small, so we used a lot of replicates. The data-set was described in Table 1.

## Methods

Primary osteoblasts were isolated from the calvaria of neonatal Sprague-Dawley rats and cultured in an osteogenic medium. Cells were seeded at 5,000 cells per cm2 on gels with Young's modulus of 1, 10 or 40 kPa. Mineralized nodules were stained with Alizarin Red S and the stained area was quantified in six wells per condition (Fig. 2). In order to evaluate YAP, nuclear localisation was scored by immunofluorescence in at least 200 nuclei per gel. The the YAP inhibitor verteporfin was dosed at 1 uM. We analyse the data with a one-way ANOVA and a post-hoc test.

## Results

Mineralised nodule area on 10 kPa gels was 2.3-fold higher than on 40 kPa gels. The fraction of nuclei with nuclear YAP was 31% on 10 kPa gels and 58 % on 40 kPa gels. Verteporfin reduced nodule area on 40 kPa gels by 22%, but did not change it on 10 kPa gels. This results shows that YAP is very important for the stiffness response. In Saos-2 cells nodule area did not differ between the stiffnesses, which is a obvious limitation.

## Discussion

Our findings are completely different from the opinion that stiffer matrices always promote osteogenic differentiation.
